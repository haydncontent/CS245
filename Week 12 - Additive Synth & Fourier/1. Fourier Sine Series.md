###### notes
let x be a signal on [0,L]
	x: [0,L]->REAL
	with x(0) = 0 = x(L)
fact: we can write $$ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_nsin(\frac{n\pi t}{L}) $$is a sum of harmonic partials with fundamental freq. f_1 = 1/2L
	frequency of n-th partial? $$ f_n = \frac{\frac{n\pi t}{L}}{2\pi t} = \frac{n}{2L} $$
	amplitude of the n-th partial? $$ A_n = \frac{2}{L} \int_{0}^{L} x(t) sin(\frac{n\pi t}{L}) dt $$


###### slides
- every continous function on interval 0-L can be expressed as a sum of sines $$ f(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_nsin(\frac{n\pi t}{L}) $$
  provided that f(0) = 0 and f(L) = 0
- the coefficients are given (uniquely) by $$ A_n = \frac{2}{L} \int_0^{L} [f(x)sin(n\pi x/L) ]dx $$
- this follows from the basic orthogonality relation for sine functions $$ \int_0^L sin(m\pi x/L)sin(n\pi x/L)dx=L/2 \gamma_{mn} $$
- for a function of L = N/R seconds sampled at rate R $$ A_n \text{ approx}= \frac{2}{N} \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f(k/R)sin(\pi n k/N) $$
- the sum may require too many terms to be an accurate approximation
- it is an inefficient way to construct anything other than simple string-like sounds
